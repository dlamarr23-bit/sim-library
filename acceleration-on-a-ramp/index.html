How to use this simulation

Tap to open the step-by-step directions.

⌄
  1. Use the height slider to change how tall the ramp is. Watch the tall ruler next to the stand — it shows exactly how high the release point is.
  2. Click the mass buttons (+50 g, +100 g, +200 g) to add weight to the cart. You can click more than one at a time.
  3. Put Photogate A and Photogate B anywhere on the ramp. Drag the posts right on the ramp, or use the position sliders. Try one gate near the top and one near the bottom.
  4. Click Release cart. It slides down from the fixed starting point. Each photogate records the time since release and how fast the cart was going the moment it passed.
  5. Use the formula a = (v₂ − v₁) ÷ (t₂ − t₁) with the two readings to figure out the acceleration between the gates by hand. Type your answer in the check box to see if you're right.
  6. Click Add trial to data table to save your run. Then change the height, mass, or gate spots and try again. Look at the a column — what stays the same, and what changes?
  7. Click Reset cart to send the cart back to the top without erasing your saved data.

The model behind the apparatus

Tap to open the formulas and the science behind them.

⌄

Why a taller ramp speeds things up more

a = g · (h / L)

The ramp is always L = 100 cm long. When you raise the release point to height h, you make the ramp steeper. A steeper ramp pulls the cart down faster, so a taller ramp always means a bigger acceleration — and that acceleration stays the same all the way down that ramp.

Finding the acceleration between two gates

a = (v₂ − v₁) ÷ (t₂ − t₁)

Each photogate gives you a speed and a time. It shouldn't matter which two spots you pick — near the top, near the bottom, or in the middle — this formula should give you close to the same number every time, because the ramp speeds up the cart by the same amount everywhere along it.

What extra mass does — and doesn't do

a ≠ f(m)

Try the same ramp height with no extra mass, then with 200 g added, using the same two gates. Since there's no friction, gravity speeds up every part of the cart by the same amount. So the acceleration should come out about the same, even though the cart is heavier. Extra mass makes the cart harder to stop — it doesn't make it speed up faster.
