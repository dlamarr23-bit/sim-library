Density Block Lab

8 stations, 8 materials: measure with a scale and a ruler, then find volume and density.

Lab directions

In this lab you will find the density of 8 different materials. You will see if the number of blocks changes a material's density.

What you need

  • 4 blocks of one material (they are already at each station)
  • A digital scale (measures mass in grams, g)
  • A metric ruler (measures length in centimeters, cm)
  • A calculator

How the stations work

There are 8 stations in the room. Each station has its own set of blocks, and each set is made of a different material.

  • Your group starts at one station.
  • When your teacher says rotate, move to the next station number. Station 8 moves to Station 1.
  • Every rotation brings a new material. Keep going until your group has visited all 8 stations.
  • On the Data side, tap the button for the station you are at. Each one has its own table.

Procedure (do this at every station)

  1. Find the mass. Put 1 block on the digital scale. Read the mass in grams (g). Type it in the Mass box.
  2. Measure the block. Use the metric ruler to measure the length, width and height in centimeters (cm). Line the edge of the block up with the 0 mark, not the end of the ruler.
  3. Find the volume. Multiply: Volume = Length × Width × Height. Type it in the Volume box. Units: cm³.
  4. Find the density. Divide: Density = Mass ÷ Volume. Type it in the Density box. Units: g/cm³.
  5. Add more blocks. Stack 2 blocks together and treat the stack like one big block. Find its mass and measure it. Do the same with 3 blocks and 4 blocks.
  6. Clean up and rotate. Put all 4 blocks back for the next group. Move to the next station when your teacher says so.
The Volume and Density boxes turn green when your math matches your measurements, yellow when you are close (check your rounding), and red when the math needs another try.

When all 8 stations are done, answer the Analysis questions.

Density and volume basics

Mass

Mass is the amount of matter (stuff) in an object. We measure mass in grams (g) with a scale.

Volume

Volume is the amount of space an object takes up. For a block, count how many 1 cm cubes would fit inside it.

V = L × W × HVolume = length × width × height

The units multiply too: cm × cm × cm = cm³ (say "cubic centimeters").

Density

Density is how much mass is packed into each cubic centimeter of a material. Two blocks can be the same size but have different masses. The one with more mass packed in has the higher density.

D = M ÷ VDensity = mass ÷ volume

Density units are g/cm³ (say "grams per cubic centimeter"). It tells you how many grams are in each 1 cm cube.

The density triangle

Cover the letter you want to find. What is left shows the math. M is over V, so you divide. D and V sit side by side, so you multiply.

D = M ÷ Vfind density
M = D × Vfind mass
V = M ÷ Dfind volume

Worked example

A block is 3 cm long, 2 cm wide and 2 cm tall. Its mass is 30.0 g.
Volume = 3 × 2 × 2 = 12 cm³
Density = 30.0 ÷ 12 = 2.5 g/cm³

Rounding: round volume to the tenths place (like 12.4) and density to the hundredths place (like 2.58).

Will it float?

The density of water is 1 g/cm³. A solid with a density less than 1 g/cm³ floats in water. A solid with a density greater than 1 g/cm³ sinks.

Words to know

Mass
The amount of matter in an object, in grams (g).
Volume
The amount of space an object takes up, in cubic centimeters (cm³).
Density
The amount of mass in each unit of volume, in grams per cubic centimeter (g/cm³).
Material
The kind of matter an object is made of, like brass, wood or PVC.

How to use this lab
  1. Tap the station button for the station you are at. Each color is a different material.
  2. Type the mass from the scale and the length, width and height from the ruler.
  3. Work out the volume (L × W × H) and the density (M ÷ V) on your calculator. Type your answers.
  4. Watch the colors: green means your math is right, yellow means check your rounding, red means try again. The black bar tells you what to fix.
  5. Fill in the rows for 1, 2, 3 and 4 blocks. The graph adds your points as you type.
  6. When you rotate, tap the next station's button. A check mark shows each station you finished.
  7. Your work saves on this device. Use Clear this station only if you want to start that station over.
