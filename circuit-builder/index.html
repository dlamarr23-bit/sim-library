Circuit Builder: Complete the Loop

Big idea
Electric current only flows through a complete (closed) circuit: an unbroken loop of conductors from one end of the battery, through the bulb, and back to the other end.

Challenges

    Must-know ideas (NGSS)

    MS-PS3 Energy transfer. A battery stores chemical potential energy. In a complete circuit, the battery pushes charges through the wires. The current carries energy to the bulb. The bulb changes it into light and heat.

    MS-PS2-3 Electric current is the flow of electric charge. In metal wires, the moving charges are tiny negative particles called electrons. The electrons are already in the wire. The battery gives them a push.

    Closed circuit vs. open circuit. A closed circuit is a full loop with no gaps, so current flows. An open circuit has a gap or an insulator somewhere in the loop, so current stops everywhere, not just at the gap.

    A switch is a planned gap. Closing it completes the loop. Opening it breaks the loop.

    PS1.A Conductors and insulators. How well a material lets current pass is a property of that material. A conductor lets current flow easily (most metals, and graphite). An insulator blocks current (plastic, glass, rubber, ceramic, dry paper).

    Looks can fool you. Shiny or gold-colored does not always mean metal. Test it!

    Crosscutting Systems and models. The battery, wires, and bulb work together as a system. This sim is a model. The blue dots show electrons, which are far too small to see in real life.

    Short circuit (safety). If wire connects the two battery ends with no bulb in the path, current takes that easy path. The current gets very large. The battery and wire get hot fast. Never do this with real batteries or wall outlets.

    How to use this simulation
    1. Add parts. Drag a part from the tray onto the blue workbench. You can also tap a part to drop it in the middle.
    2. Connect ends. Every part has two ends. A dashed red circle is a loose end. Drag one end onto another end to join them.
    3. Move or turn parts. Drag the middle of a part to move it. Drag one end to turn or stretch it.
    4. Check the status bar. It tells you if the circuit is open, complete, or a short circuit. Blue dots show electrons moving.
    5. Use the switch. Tap a switch to open or close it.
    6. Test items. The test items are in the tray below the switch. Predict: conductor or insulator? Then put an item in your loop in place of one wire. Connect a wire end right to each side of the item and watch the bulb.
    7. Fix mistakes. Tap a part to delete it. Tap a joined end to disconnect it. Reset bench starts over.
