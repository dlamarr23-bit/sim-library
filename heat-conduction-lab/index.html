Thermal Equilibrium ⌄

Heat moves from hot things to cold things. It never moves the other way by itself. The bigger the temperature gap between the beakers, the faster heat moves. As the beakers get closer to the same temperature, heat moves more slowly. That's why each curve is steep at first and flattens out later.

Both beakers are sealed and insulated, so no heat can escape into the room. Whatever heat Beaker A loses, Beaker B gains. That means if you add the two temperatures together, you always get 100°C. Eventually the beakers reach the same temperature, 50°C. Scientists call this thermal equilibrium.

Conductors & Insulators ⌄

A thermal conductor lets heat move through it quickly. A thermal insulator slows heat down. Scientists measure this with a number called thermal conductivity, or k, measured in watts per meter-kelvin (W/m·K). A bigger k means heat moves faster through that material.

How to use this simulation ⌄
  1. Pick a material — Aluminum, Copper, Steel, or Glass — using the buttons above the beakers. This puts both beakers back to their starting temperatures.
  2. Press Play to start the simulation. Beaker A starts at 95°C and Beaker B starts at 5°C, joined by a sealed rod made of the material you picked.
  3. Watch the thermometers and the glowing dots inside the tube. The dots move faster when the temperature difference is big, and slower as the beakers get closer in temperature.
  4. Tap Bar Chart to see both temperatures side by side right now.
  5. Tap Graph to see how each temperature changes over time. Move your mouse over the graph to check the exact numbers at any second.
  6. Tap Table to see every temperature reading, saved automatically every 20 seconds. Use these numbers for your worksheet.
  7. Tap Pause to freeze the simulation, or Reset to start over with a different material.
