How to use this simulation
  1. Choose One sled or Two sleds at the top of the picture.
  2. Set where each sled is released: move its Release height slider, type a height in the box, or drag the sled up or down the hill. The release height can be 0 to 50 m. Its Height also shows in the bottom corner.
  3. Set each sled's mass with its slider, or type a number in the box. The mass can be 10 to 200 kg.
  4. With one sled, use the Snowmen slider to line up 0 to 5 snowmen. With two sleds, choose Sleds stick together or Sleds bounce.
  5. Check Show energy to see each sled's potential energy and kinetic energy while it moves.
  6. Press Play (the triangle). The speed gauge shows how fast each sled is going. With one sled, the Collision result box tells you what happened in the crash.
  7. Press Pause to freeze the action. Press Reset to put the sleds back. You must reset before you can change the setup.
Where does the energy go?

Potential energy (PE) is stored energy. Here it comes from height. A sled high on the hill has a lot of it. More height or more mass means more potential energy.

Kinetic energy (KE) is the energy of motion. A faster sled has more kinetic energy. So does a heavier sled going the same speed.

As the sled slides down, its potential energy changes into kinetic energy. At the bottom, all of it is kinetic energy. This hill has no friction, so the total energy stays the same the whole way down.

The speed at the bottom depends only on the height. A 50 kg sled and a 200 kg sled that start at 25 m both reach 22.14 m/s. The heavier sled has more energy, though.

It takes 20,000 J (joules) to knock down one snowman. Each snowman takes some of the sled's kinetic energy. When the energy runs out, the sled stops. A sled with more energy wrecks more snowmen.

Crashing two sleds

Momentum is mass times velocity. Velocity is speed in a direction. Here, moving right counts as plus and moving left counts as minus.

When the sleds crash, the total momentum before the crash equals the total momentum after it. This is called conservation of momentum.

Sleds stick together: the sleds move as one after the crash. Some kinetic energy turns into heat and sound, so there is less kinetic energy after the crash. Two sleds with the same mass and speed stop dead.

Sleds bounce: the sleds push off each other like springs. No kinetic energy is lost. A light sled hit by a heavy sled can fly away fast, even over the top of the hill.

The math the simulation uses
  • Potential energy: PE = m × g × h
  • Kinetic energy: KE = ½ × m × v²
  • Use m in kilograms, h in meters, v in meters per second, and g = 9.8 N/kg. Then the energy comes out in joules (J).
  • Speed at the bottom of the hill (no friction): v = √(2 × g × h)
  • Snowmen destroyed: KE ÷ 20,000 J (up to the number of snowmen)
  • Momentum: p = m × v
  • Sleds stick together: v = (m₁v₁ + m₂v₂) ÷ (m₁ + m₂)
  • Sleds bounce: the total kinetic energy after the crash equals the total before it.
